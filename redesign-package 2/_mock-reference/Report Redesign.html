What's working against it today

Six issues a redesign needs to fix

Now
Fragmented rhythm. Every chart sits alone in its own block. Related metrics drift apart instead of being read together; the page feels like a slideshow scrolling past, not a report.
Redesign
Banded modules. Group net worth + by-age + distribution into one read. Each major theme is a single horizontal band with internal grid, not five vertical blocks.
Now
No typographic hierarchy. Section titles, chart titles, and metrics all read at similar weight, so the eye can't find the headline number on the page.
Redesign
Three-tier scale. Eyebrow → section title at 44px Medium → 56–96px display numbers for headline stats. Hierarchy carried by size, not weight or color.
Now
Charts look generic. Default chart-library aesthetics — thick gridlines, primary colors, tooltips on hover. They read as Plotly, not as part of a designed report.
Redesign
House style for every chart. One accent color (purple), tabular numerals, dotted 1px gridlines, axis labels at 11px tertiary, no chart titles — the section title is the chart title.
Now
No narrative spine. Sections are ordered by question number, not by what a reader wants to know. The "26% already FI" headline gets buried alongside everything else.
Redesign
Five-act structure. Who responded → where they stand → where they're going → how they live → the FI cohort. Each act opens with one headline number.
Now
Some charts are the wrong choice. Bar charts for things that should be distributions; pies for things with too many categories; lines for things that aren't time series.
Redesign
Pick the viz to the question. Distributions → histograms with median callout. Multi-category breakdowns → ranked bar lists. By-age trends → connected scatter with band, not a flat bar.
Now
Mobile feels like an afterthought. Tables and side-by-side charts squeeze, axis labels collide, and the page becomes a long column of cramped widgets.
Redesign
Designed at two widths. Multi-column grids collapse to ranked lists with inline bars. Charts re-flow with new label density. Density stays high — nothing balloons.
Principles for the redesign

Eight rules the rebuild follows

01 — Color

Navy on white, with one purple.

The whole report reads as navy on near-white. Purple is the only accent and appears at most once per screen — for the headline number, an active state, or the hero gradient. No chart-library palettes.

02 — Type

Hierarchy by size, not by weight.

Medium (500) is the working weight for headlines. Distinguish levels by size and tracking — 44px section titles, 56–96px display numbers — rather than by going bold or changing color.

03 — Numbers

One headline number per section.

Every section opens with the single number a reader will remember. The chart is supporting evidence, not the main event. Big numerals are tracked tight (-0.035em) and use tabular figures throughout.

04 — Density

Pack related metrics; pad between themes.

Inside a theme (net worth + by age + distribution) cards sit on a tight 24px grid. Between themes, sections breathe — 56px vertical rhythm and a 1px slate rule separator. The "fragmented" feel comes from doing the opposite.

05 — Charts

House style applied to every chart.

1px dotted gridlines in slate-050. Axis labels in 11px tertiary. No chart titles — the section title is the title. Medians and percentiles drawn as labeled lines, not annotations in legends.

06 — Voice

Keep the community; lose the cuteness.

The Reddit-community origin is part of the story. Keep the plain-spoken framing and self-reported caveats — but cut emoji, asides, and meme adjacency. Confident sentence-case headlines.

07 — Imagery

Gradient mesh on hero. White everywhere else.

The hero earns one full-bleed gradient. Every content section is white or slate-025. No stock photos, no decorative illustrations, no patterns. Charts and numbers carry visual interest.

08 — Mobile

Tables become ranked lists with inline bars.

Three-column grids → single column with each row using a CSS bar to show its share. Charts re-flow to vertical orientation. Density holds; the page should not balloon on a phone.
